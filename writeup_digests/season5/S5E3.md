## S5E3 — consensus recipe
- **Architecture distribution:** 2nd `FLAT` (equal-weight 6-model blend; the medal-winning component was ONE SVC, but the
  submission that earned the rank was the blend) · 6th `FLAT` (2-branch XGB, 0.7/0.3 weighted) · 18th `SINGLE`
  (XGB + custom AUC loss; early FLAT blend abandoned) · 37th `SINGLE` (TabPFN).
  - **winner topology (of covered entries): `FLAT`** — and the 1st-place score in this competition (0.90728, private) is
    itself documented on the 2nd's page as a 3-model EQUAL-WEIGHT blend: no entry in this set used a trained meta-learner.
- **New architectures at the board:** `TabPFN` twice — 37th's entire winning single model (params not stated) and one
  blend member at 2nd (CV 0.894 / private 0.90193 solo). No FT-Transformer/TabM/etc. appears. 18th's custom AUC loss
  is a training-objective change, not a new architecture.
- **Agreed on (3 of 4 — S5E3-02, S5E3-06, S5E3-18):** year-structured or Deotte-derived fold CV as the backbone: 2nd
  GroupKFold 6 folds by `id//365`; 18th Kfold(6) "equivalent to groupkfold by year"; 6th "same inner fold outer fold
  structure as Chirs D. last month". 37th publishes no CV scheme (S5E3-37 excluded).
- **Agreed on (3 of 4 — S5E3-02, S5E3-06, S5E3-18):** public LB is unreliable at ~146 rows — trust CV/private: 2nd
  "mostly ignore public LB" + equal weights; 6th's scheme looked bad on public and "surprisingly did very well" on private;
  18th selected submissions by max CV−public gap. 37th is silent (excluded).
- **Agreed on (2 of 4 — S5E3-02, S5E3-06):** exploit the ORIGINAL dataset as modeling signal — 2nd via concat-rows and
  merge-columns views (the orig-as-columns SVC alone scored private 0.90610 = his medal component), 6th via a dedicated
  XGB branch at 0.3 weight. Divergent: 18th simulated adding it and rejected it (50% of cases helped); 37th excluded it
  by design ("generated from the same LLM" argument).
- **Divergences:** FE philosophy split the board: 2nd did ZERO FE on principle (366 effective rows) and won the medal;
  6th and 18th both engineered (group-share stats / cloud-value features + forward selection) and finished 6th/18th;
  37th kept only "basic FE". The published private scores separate the top: 0.90728 (1st, cdeotte's unsubmitted 3-blend)
  vs 0.90604 (2nd) vs 0.90395 (18th) — a 0.00333 spread — while 2nd's own extra models raised CV 0.898→0.901 yet lost
  private 0.90728→0.90604 (−0.00124): CV gains beyond the core blend were worthless on this data.
- **Highest-leverage single trick:** treating the synthetic set as 366 real rows and folding the ORIGINAL dataset into
  the features (one grouped-mean column per feature, `train[c].map(orig.groupby(c).rainfall.mean())`) — it produced the
  strongest single model (RAPIDS SVC, private 0.90610) and the 1st-place-level 3-blend (S5E3-02).
- **Nothing worked:** adding more blend members once the core 3 are in (2nd: CV +0.002/+0.003, private −0.00124);
  day/seasonality FE on raw columns (commenter Demon Sheriff, 2nd's page: "only made my model worse"); RFE-scale FE
  search (18th: cost); training on the appended public dataset (18th: helped in only 50% of simulations); chasing
  public LB at all in this shakeup (2nd, 6th, 18th agree).
