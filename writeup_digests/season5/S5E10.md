- **TL;DR:** A 3-level stack (many models → Keras-NN / Lasso ensembles → CatBoost secondary ensembler → hill climb over 4
  ensemble artifacts), where the two things he credits are 11 genetic-programming features at the **ensembling** stage and
  CatBoost-as-secondary-ensembler.
- Sub-0.00002 deltas decide the whole board: "Other than the top 4 spots 'tied' at 0.05563, the next ~200 competitors were all
  at 0.05564."
- **Architecture:** STACKN · stages=3 · l1=~40-50 models per ensembler run (XGB incl. residual boosting, CatBoost, TabM ×3
  tuned parameter sets, Keras FM, TF-embedding NNs, PyTorch-Tabular nets, Lasso, AutoGluon) ·
  l2=Keras NN ensembles + Lasso ensembles; then CatBoost as a **secondary** ensembler seeded with the Keras ensemble as
  `baseline` (automatic residual boosting) · l3=greedy hill climbing over 4 of those level-2 artifacts = the submission ·
  novel=TabM · mod=gp,embed ·
  topo=data views [Mix / all-Categorical / all-Numerical + Autoencoder latents + 11 GP features] → 40-50 models →
  Keras-NN / Lasso ensemble [4 artifacts, 1 of them re-ensembled by CatBoost-with-baseline + 11 GP features] → hill climb(4) → submitted
  - Three trained levels, so `STACKN` not `STACK2`: the CatBoost secondary ensembler is itself fitted on the outputs of the
    Keras level, and the top level blends four already-ensembled artifacts.
  - The CatBoost `baseline` step is genuinely forward data flow (his words: "essentially the same as boosting residuals except
    that adding and subtracting from the target is done automatically for us"), which makes `CASCADE` a legitimate reading of
    that one stage; the tag follows the structure that produced the submission, which is the stack.
  - `mod=gp`: the 11 GP features are **features of the level-2 blender**, not a level of their own — the author states they
    bought 0.00001 as an ensemble input and nothing as individual models. `mod=embed`: Autoencoder latent-space
    representations were fed in as features after about day 10 (he reports they did not work well alone).
  - The winning hill-climb output "looks the same over 5 decimals as the best individual ensemble", so the rank was decided by
    which of 12 indistinguishable files he uploaded (he picked the 2nd and 5th best).
- **Setup:** he had a three-week October window; "my computers could attest to that".
- Structural quirk exploited: the target is generated by a closed-form equation, and he prints it (see Features) next to the
  Lasso-fitted approximation of it.
- **Features:** four data views (author's flowchart row 1): `Mix` (original data holding both categorical and numerical
  features), all-`Categorical`, all-`Numerical`, plus after ~10 days `Autoencoder` latent-space representations and
  `Genetic programming` derived features.
  - 11 GP-derived features, added **only at the ensembling stage**; published as a notebook whose files drop straight into a
    pipeline. Example, verbatim:
    `train['GP_11'] = np.sqrt(np.abs(np.sin(np.sin(np.sin(np.sin(np.sin(np.sin(0.254397*train['speed']*np.sqrt(np.abs(train['lighting']*train['speed']*np.exp(train['curvature'])*np.sqrt(np.abs(train['lighting']**3*np.sin(train['speed']**2*train['accidents']*np.cos(train['weather']))/train['weather']))))))))))))`
  - GP runs were restricted to relevant columns: "even here only the relevant features were selected for calculation."
  - Two arithmetic-only GP runs ([+, -, *, /] allowed) produced near-exact recaptures of the generator, verbatim:
    `0.308*[curvature] + 0.180*[speed_limit_3] + 0.180*[speed_limit_4] + 0.128*[num_reported_accidents_4] + 0.180*[lighting_2] + 0.107*[weather_1] + 0.118*[weather_2] - 0.0004`
    and `0.309*[curvature] + 0.186*[speed_limit_3] + 0.186*[speed_limit_4] + 0.0929*[num_reported_accidents_4] + 0.186*[lighting_2] + 0.099*[weather_1] + 0.0929*[weather_2] + 0.011`
  - The generator's own target equation, quoted by him: `base_risk = 0.3*curvature + 0.2*(lighting == 'night') +
    0.1*(weather != 'clear') + 0.2*(speed_limit >= 60) + 0.1*(num_reported_accidents > 2)`.
  - The Lasso-fitted version (coefficients with |value| < 0.05 ignored), verbatim: `0.3046*curvature + 0.0706*lighting_2
    - 0.1203*lighting_0 - 0.1181*lighting_1 - 0.0928*weather_0 - 0.1232*speed_limit_0 - 0.1232*speed_limit_2
    - 0.1218*speed_limit_1 + 0.0646*speed_limit_4 + 0.0629*speed_limit_3 + 0.0789*num_reported_accidents_4
    + 0.0704*num_reported_accidents_5`. He calls Lasso coefficients "extremely informative in this competition".
  - Columns named across the writeup: `curvature`, `lighting`, `weather`, `speed_limit`, `num_reported_accidents`, `speed`,
    `accidents`. Dropped columns, encodings, seeds: not stated.

## S5E10 — consensus recipe
- **Architecture distribution:** 1st `STACKN` (40-50 models → Keras-NN/Lasso ensembles → CatBoost secondary ensembler → hill
  climb of 4) · 3rd `STACKN` (5 base → stacking NN → YDF meta on L2-out + base features → 50/50) ·
  4th `STACK2` (14 XGB → NN meta-blender, 17 seeds → hill climb; residual-XGB is a `CASCADE`-type recipe inside level 1) ·
  5th `STACK2` (TabM OOF-as-features → stacked XGB, hill climb of 7, `mod=pseudo`) ·
  7th `STACK2` (8 members → Ridge meta, 15-fold) · 8th `STACK2` (~40 OOF columns pruned to 4-6 → RidgeCV) ·
  14th `FLAT` (3 greedy HC ensembles + 1 Ridge stack, averaged; `mod=neg-weights`).
  - **winner topology: `STACKN`** — 2 of 7 stack three or more trained levels (S5E10-01, S5E10-03), 4 of 7 use a two-level
    stack with a linear, NN or GBM meta (S5E10-04, S5E10-05, S5E10-07, S5E10-08), and the single `FLAT` entry finished last
    (S5E10-14, private 0.05564).
  - Read-across on metas: the trained meta-learners at the top of the stacks are small and mixed — Keras NN (1st, 3rd, 4th),
    Lasso (1st), CatBoost secondary ensembler (1st — his biggest single move, though he calls that route the slow one:
    4-10 hour Optuna runs), YDF (3rd), stacked XGB (5th), Ridge (7th, 8th). 1st says he moved to NN-only ensembling after
    ~2 weeks because it "worked better than anything else and was reasonably fast".
- **New architectures at the board:** `TabM` is on 6 of 7 boards (1st tuned it further and published 3 better parameter sets;
  3rd used it as 2 of 5 base members including a residual variant; 5th made it his entire level 1; 7th and 8th each ran it as
  a member; 14th lists it in his NN group; only 4th is TabM-free) — but on this page set nobody publishes a TabM-only score,
  so it is a member, not a winner.
  - `YDF` (Yahoo Decision Forests) reaches the deepest of any novel model: 3rd used it as his **level-3 meta-learner**, the
    only novel model in the file occupying a meta position; 7th and 14th ran it as a level-1 member.
  - `RealMLP` appears only in 14th's NN group; `LAMA`, `Trompt`, `GANDALF` and the rest of the S5E11 zoo are absent here.
  - `AutoGluon` shows up twice and never pays: 1st "produced many promising models, but none of them were helping the
    ensemble"; 8th ran aliffaagnur's AutoGluon Adapter as one base member (verdict not stated); per ruling 6 neither is an
    `AUTOML` submission.
