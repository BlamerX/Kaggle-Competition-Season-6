### S5E1-02 · 2nd · cdeotte (Chris Deotte) · LB 0.04526/0.04498 · CV not stated

- **TL;DR:** Linear-regression decomposition predicts the past; a transformer is then trained on the
  prediction *error* and the two are summed — residual stacking, no blender.
- Year-level multiplier choice matters more for LB than day-level accuracy: "choosing the right multipliers
  for the future years ... has a greater effect on your final LB score and rank than having a model which
  predicts days accurately."
- **Architecture:** CASCADE · stages=2 · l1=1 LR decomposition + 1 transformer (median of 5 seeds in the
  transformer branch) · l2=none (final = sum of the two predictions, not a trained meta) ·
  novel=tabular transformer (autoregressive) · mod=pseudo ·
  topo=LR(+GDP, store, product, country, DoW, DoY) -> truth-minus-LR-prediction on train window -> transformer trained on that error -> submit LR prediction + transformer error prediction
  - Stage 1 output becomes stage 2's target (residual boosting) — exactly ruling 5's `CASCADE`, even though
    the author titles the section "Stacking"; he points to discussion 560853 for his stacking-vs-ensemble distinction.
  - Transformer is autoregressive/recursive multi-step; LR branch is direct multi-step with the sinusoidal
    features extended into the future years.
  - Global model: "train 1 model on all 5 products" — one transformer across the series, not per-series models.
  - `mod=pseudo` applies to the stacked winner itself, not just the transformer-only recipe: the author's own
    comment confirms 2 pseudo-label rounds on the LR residual target (median of 5 seeds kept in every stage).
- **Setup:** train = years 2010-2016, predict = 2017, 2018, 2019; 90 numbers per day per country x store x
  product combination; 2 final slots used for the multiplier bets below.
- Structural quirk: prediction error of the top public notebooks (future-year multiplier needed) ranges
  roughly +/- 6% around 1.00.
- **Features:** LR branch: sinusoidal (day-of-year) engineered features plus effects of GDP, store, product,
  country, day of week, day of year.
- Transformer branch: 30 boolean holiday features (one per holiday).
- Holiday EDA: black vertical lines before/after each holiday per country, check across 2010-2016 whether
  sales are consistently raised/lowered, keep it if so. Singapore's 7 sales-raising holidays named: Chinese
  New Year, Easter, Vesak Day, National Day, Eid al-Fitr, Deepavali, Eid al-Adha.
- Other engineered columns, dropped columns: not stated.
- **Models:** Linear regression (sinusoidal + factor effects) — starting point Konstantin's public model-1

- **Ensembling:** not a blender — cascade: transformer trained on target = truth minus LR prediction over
  2010-2016; final submission = LR prediction + transformer prediction. Seed combination on this page:
  "use the median of 5 models trained with different seeds" for each prediction stage.
- 2 final slots: `constant m=1.06` (green dotted option in his figure) and `mild linear up`
  (`m = 1.06 + slope * (year - 2017)`); which slot took 2nd is not stated.
- **Post-processing:** the future-year multiplier is itself the post-processing: options m=1.00, constant

- **Features:** the 8 base factors (author's list): GDP per capita; store ratio; country ratio; periodic

  day-of-year factor.
- Holidays via the `holidays` library: each holiday x country = one indicator column; `normal` and `observed`

  holidays fall on the same day.

- Additional economic factors: tried, all failed except GDP per capita.
- **Models:** no off-the-shelf ML model at all: `scipy.optimize.minimize` fits alpha parameters of
