### S5E12-02 · 2nd · daylighth (DaylightH) · LB 0.70502 (public, stated by commenter wind1234it; author's unselected best PB 0.70503)/not stated · CV 0.70771 (Cutoff/Bagged-Hill-Climbing ensemble AUC)

- **Link:** https://www.kaggle.com/c/playground-series-s5e12/writeups/2nd-place-solution-winning-based-on-id-shift-an
- **Status:** FETCHED (first attempt, `/competitions/` form, body + comments)
- **TL;DR:** Won by structurally exploiting the ID shift: the train tail (~last 3% by id) matches the test distribution,
  so CV = tail validation and all modeling is weighted toward tail + original data.
- Submission = Bagged Hill Climbing (50 bags × 2000 iters) over ~15 hand-picked diverse members.
- **Architecture:** FLAT · stages=1 · l1=15 models (the table: 4 LightGBM, 5 XGBoost, 2 CatBoost, 2 NN, tail-LR, tail-GAM; count from his table) · l2=none (hill-climbing weights, not a trained meta — Ridge positive variant tested at 0.70751 but 0.70771 HC was submitted) ·
  novel=none (pool contains a DAE member and a stacked-NN member, both his own, no named new architecture) ·
  mod=pseudo,adv (pseudo-label members `lgb_pseudo`/`xgb_pl_soft`; adversarial validation drives the whole setup) ·
  topo=train tail + original data (weighted tail 16x / orig 8x / head 1x) → 15 diverse members (incl. pseudo-labeled and residual variants) → Bagged Hill Climbing (50 bags × 2000 iters, Cutoff-AUC objective) → submit
  - FLAT per ruling 1: bagged hill climbing selects weights, it trains no meta-learner. The Ridge alternative
    (positive-constrained) is the trained-meta variant he tested and did not use for the submission (0.70751 vs 0.70771).
  - Two members are internally multi-stage but enter the blend as single columns: `nn_stacking` (stacked NN whose input
    is OOF predictions, cutoff AUC 0.70616 — the best single member) and `xgb_residual` (boosting over a fixed base
    margin derived from the Tail Mean, 0.70474). Pseudo-label members (`lgb_pseudo`, `xgb_pl_soft`) are level-0
    variants → loop = `mod=pseudo` per ruling 9, not primary.
- **Setup:** rows/cols from his analysis output: train head 677,000 vs tail 23,000 rows; validation = `id >= 678260`
  (approx last 3% of train, his footnote). Structural quirk exploited (the core of the entry):
  - Detection: @laureanoarcanio's post (discussion/659313) showed using `id` as a feature boosts LB; @alan1305 explained
    the `id × physical_activity_minutes_per_week` rolling-mean shifts to the test distribution at the train tail.
  - Confirmation: he ran Adversarial Validation ("Micro-Scan of the Tail Transition"): LGBM discriminator (n_estimators=500,
    lr=0.05, num_leaves=31, seed 42) trained head-vs-tail; head rows scored for tail-similarity, qcut into 10 bins of
    67,700; target rate is flat ~0.64-0.655 for bins 0-6 (bin0 0.643648) then collapses in the most tail-like bins
    7→9 (0.612186 → 0.581004 → 0.521359), while the true
    Tail mean is 0.6214 → the most test-like rows carry the most WRONG label distribution = Concept Shift.
  - Exploit: validation entirely on the tail (Cutoff AUC); training forces tail+original signal, suppresses head noise.
  - Consequence for the CV story: the standard K-fold CV is replaced by last-3%-of-ids holdout; public LB is the
    post-shift region, hence the 1st-place "almost 200 spot" public→private shakeups seen across the board.
- **Features:** deliberately minimal ("I didn't perform any aggressive Feature Engineering, so I only attempted to use
  the most fundamental statistics"); statistical features from the original dataset per masayakawamata's post (discussion/648186).
  - Tail-based Target Encoding (`lgb_te`): TE computed using only Tail + Orig data.
  - Target Encoding from Original data on binned numerics (`xgb_binned`).
  - Interaction features (`lgb` baseline) and explicit interactions e.g. `gender_age` (`cat_inter`).
  - Toxic-feature drop (`lgb_safe`): removed high-concept-shift features like `physical_activity`.
  - `id` as a feature: he states in comments that adding `id` raised BOTH CV and LB — the pattern that exposed the shift.
  - Feature source kernels: none used ("almost never looked at the public notebooks in the code section").
- **Models:** per his table (Model · strategy · Cutoff AUC):

    `xgb_residual` residual boosting over fixed base margin from Tail Mean 0.70474
  - CatBoost: `cat_weighted` (same 16x/8x/1x) 0.70489 · `cat_inter` explicit interactions 0.70489
  - NN: `nn_dae` DAE + fine-tune on Tail only 0.70034 · `nn_stacking` stacked NN, input = OOF predictions 0.70616

## S5E12 — consensus recipe
- **Architecture distribution:** 1st `STACK2` (HC stage-1 selection → Ridge(alpha=10) trained on rank-transformed
  top-36 OOF preds) · 2nd `FLAT` (Bagged Hill Climbing, 50 bags × 2000 iters, over 15 members; Ridge alternative tested
  at 0.70751 and rejected) · 4th `FLAT` (manual weighted blend, TabM×2 + XGB + public prediction set).
  - **winner topology: `STACK2`** — the 1st-place submission is the only entry that trained a meta-learner (Ridge) on
    member outputs; ruling 1 is what decides this: HC alone would be FLAT, the fitted Ridge on top is a second stage.
- **New architectures at the board:** `TabM` — 4th only, as two level-0 blend members (standard + oversampled); never the
  submitted model alone, always inside the FLAT blend; its stated value is diversity/complementary signal, not solo score
  (no TabM score published on the page). No other novel architecture appears: 1st's pool is unnamed trees+NNs, 2nd's pool
  is GBDT/LR/GAM/DAE-NN/stacked-NN (classic pieces), and no entrant used a new architecture as the meta-learner (1st's
  meta is Ridge).
- **Agreed on (3 of 3 — S5E12-01, S5E12-02, S5E12-04):** the train/test ID/distribution shift is THE competition axis —
  1st runs all decisions on post-cutoff CV and names distribution shifts "competition killers"; 2nd rebuilds his whole
  validation and weighting (tail-CV `id>=678260`, tail 16x/orig 8x/head 1x) on it; 4th weights "samples after the
  cutoff ID" in XGBoost.
