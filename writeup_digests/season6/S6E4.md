### S6E4-01 · 1st · kirill0212 (cstdy) · LB not stated/not stated · CV 0.98155 (ensemble), 0.9805 (best single OvR model)

- **TL;DR:** Two binary one-vs-rest classifiers exploit the fact that the model almost never confuses Low with High; their probabilities feed a logit-space LogisticRegressionCV stack over 61 public+own OOF sets.
- **Architecture:** STACK2 · stages=2 · l1=61 OOF models (17 XGB, 11 LGBM, 10 CatBoost, 9 RealMLP, 3 TabM, 2 LogReg + a few others; 30 from @wguesdon, 6 from his own OvR build, rest public) · l2=LogisticRegressionCV on logits, 5-fold · novel=RealMLP,TabM · mod=ovo,public-oof,threshold ·
  topo=(Model1: Low vs Rest) + (Model2: Medium vs High) → P(L)/P(M)/P(H) per member → 61 OOF sets → logit-transform → LogisticRegressionCV(5-fold) → greedy threshold search on OOF balanced accuracy
  - Stage 1 is not a plain multiclass: each base model is itself two binary classifiers. Model 1 trained Low vs Rest on the whole dataset; Model 2 trained Medium vs High (labels taken from Model 1's predictions, the option he chose over true labels), OOF generated on the whole dataset.
  - The submitted prediction is the STACK (the LogisticRegressionCV over 61 OOF sets), not a single member; but every member it blends is an OvR-decomposed model.
  - Class probabilities from the two binaries: `P(Low)=P(Model_1)`, `P(Medium)=(1-P(Model_1))*(1-P(Model_2))`, `P(High)=(1-P(Model_1))*P(Model_2)`.
- **Setup:** ~630K rows, 3 classes (Low/Medium/High), High rare; 2 final submission slots; structural quirk exploited = models almost never confuse Low and High (from discussion 690677), which makes the 2-classifier decomposition valid.
- **Features:** not enumerated in the writeup; his one self-built FE + 6-model set is "based on public notebooks". He also trained extra models on other people's FE when a public notebook shipped only one model.
- **Models:** OvR XGBoost CV 0.9805; OvR RealMLP CV 0.9805 (his own two-binary build, 6 models). Pool composition: 17 XGBoost, 11 LightGBM, 10 CatBoost, 9 RealMLP, 3 TabM, 2 LogisticRegression + a few others = 61 OOF. Individual hyperparameters not stated.
- **CV:** 5-fold everywhere (he "ensured that all OOF predictions were obtained on 5 folds cv"). Ensemble CV 0.98155 vs best single 0.9805 = +0.00105 from stacking. Winner submission pub/priv not printed; comment: many submissions had private between 0.981 and 0.9815 at the same CV.
- **Ensembling:** logit transform + LogisticRegressionCV (5 folds) over the 61 OOF probability sets; the same two-binary classifier scheme was reused to blend probabilities. 30 of 61 sets are from @wguesdon.
- **Post-processing:** two greedy threshold-search algorithms, both selecting on OOF balanced accuracy (rank first, then calibrate thresholds for the metric).
- **Gains:** (+0.00105 CV) 61-model LogReg-CV stack 0.98155 over best single OvR model 0.9805.
  - (structural, unquantified vs multiclass baseline) the OvR two-binary framing itself, credited to the Low/High non-confusion observation.
- **Failed:** not stated as explicit dead ends in this writeup. Second binary option rejected: he chose Model-2-on-Model-1-predictions over training Model 2 on true Medium/High labels, but gives no number for the rejected variant.
- **Comments:** @optimistix (4th) in reply: he also trained High-vs-Medium and Medium-vs-Low binaries but only used them as features — "they went to the top, but didn't make a huge difference otherwise". Author on slot choice: he submitted 2 with different High-class predicted counts on test, and one happened to work on private; selection, not modeling, decided the medal. ## S6E4 — consensus recipe
- **Architecture distribution:** 1st `STACK2` (ovo: two-binary OvR classifiers → LogisticRegressionCV over 61 OOF) · 2nd `STACK2` + `AGENT` (GPU PyTorch multinomial LogReg stacker over 125 L1, pool ported by Claude/Codex) · 3rd `STACK2` (LightGBM multiclass stacker over ~168 of 203 OOF + guarded voting) · 4th `STACKN` (up to 11 L1 ensemblers → up to 4 L2 ensemblers, voted) · 5th `STACK2` + `AGENT` (multiclass LightGBM stacker over 119 pruned models, Codex/Claude experiment loop) · 12th `STACK2` (LightGBM stack over 41 + log-bias argmax) · 19th `STACK2` (RidgeClassification meta over 29, meta sees probs+class+formula) · 24th `STACKN` (blend of RealMLP + TabFPN + TabM neural stackers over 166 OOF) · 30th `STACK2` (LogisticRegression-on-logits meta over a heterogeneous pool). Winner topology: `STACK2` — 7 of 9 entrants (1st, 2nd, 3rd, 5th, 12th, 19th, 30th) submitted a single trained linear/GBM meta-learner over an L1 OOF matrix; the two `STACKN` entrants (4th, 24th) and the winner all share the same "stack the stackers / stack many OOFs" idea.
- **New architectures at the board:** only ONE entrant put a novel architecture at level 2: 24th stacked his 166-OOF matrix with RealMLP / TabFPN / TabM neural meta-models (TabFPN is his own stacker). Everywhere else level-2 is LightGBM (3rd, 5th, 12th), LogisticRegression (1st, 30th, and 2nd's GPU PyTorch multinomial), or Ridge (19th). Novel models otherwise appear only as L1 members: `RealMLP` is everywhere (1st, 3rd, 5th, 12th, 19th, 24th all ship it), `TabM` (1st, 3rd, 5th, 12th, 24th), `Trompt` (3rd), `TabTransformer` (5th, 24th), `FT-Transformer` (5th), `H2OAutoML` (3rd, 1 model). Best single-model CVs by a novel arch: RealMLP 0.9805 (1st, tied with XGB), RealMLP 0.97802 (12th), TabM/RealMLP 0.9798 range (5th); the exotic screens that failed: TabPFN (12th, hit 12h limit at 630K rows), GraphSAGE GNN (3rd and 12th both excluded it), YDF (3rd excluded, 24th discarded).
- **Agreed on (5 of 9 did it successfully; 3 more tried and it hurt) - the core primitive:** the multiclass problem is decomposed or argmax-tuned for balanced accuracy because High is only 3.3%. 1st used an explicit OvR two-binary decomposition + greedy threshold search; 30th tuned LR to `class_weight='balanced'` + `max_p` headroom on logits; 12th applied log-bias class shifts; 3rd applied guarded Medium↔High voting; 2nd weighted classes inside the multinomial stacker. 19th and 24th both TRIED bias/threshold tuning and reported it raised CV while lowering private LB; 4th attacked the label metric with L2 voting rather than class weights; 5th stated no argmax step.
- **Agreed on (7 of 9):** a LEARNED linear/GBM meta-learner over an L1 OOF matrix — LogisticRegression (1st CV-stack, 30th, and 2nd's PyTorch multinomial) or LightGBM (3rd, 5th, 12th) or Ridge (19th). 24th broke the pattern by stacking with NEURAL nets (RealMLP/TabFPN/TabM) and blending them.
- **Agreed on (5 of 9):** feed the stacker probabilities (or logits) and nothing else — the L1 pool sees features, the meta sees only predictions (3rd "did not use raw features directly", 12th "LightGBM stacker uses OOF class probabilities", 5th "probabilities used as features", same in 1st and 2nd). 19th is the deliberate counter-example: he added each member's hard predicted class AND Chris Deotte's exact-formula column to the meta matrix.
- **Agreed on (5 of 9, and the top lever where stated):** Ordered Target Encoding / digit-extraction / discretized-numeric features dominate FE. 12th: OTE with 4x shuffle = single biggest lever (+0.001 CV); 5th: target-encoding + rounded/digit + original-rule features; 24th: KBinsDiscretizer (n_bins=80, uniform/quantile/kmeans) drove his best XGB; 19th: nested target encoding + digit features; 3rd: floor-categoricals + quantile bins.
- **Agreed on (4 of 9, the community effect):** public OOF/npy prediction sets are a first-class source. 1st took 30 of 61 sets from wguesdon; 3rd assembled a ~2GB archive of public `.npy` (53 named public contributions, 30 of them wguesdon's); 24th's 166-OOF pool far exceeds the 52 models he trained himself and ships as a public "80-20 public-private OOFs and preds" dataset, plus he used ravi20076's public blender; 5th carried 1 "public/external ensemble" member and credited public notebooks. Only wguesdon's own 12th-place writeup names no external OOF set — he published his instead, and Optimistix (4th) credits that release on the 12th thread with "keeping my ensembles small this month".
- **Agreed on (5 of 9 named an LLM tool):** Claude Code / Codex as the build accelerator — 2nd ported 150 scripts in one day, 5th ran a 24/7 Codex experiment queue (these two are the only ones whose pipeline architecture the agent itself shaped), while 4th, 12th and 30th used Claude Code for coding, notebook ports and co-piloting without it being the primary architecture.
- **Agreed on (the CV-is-broken warning, 6 of 9):** on this board CV↔public↔private are weakly correlated. 3rd: CV 0.98172 → LB 0.98126 but CV 0.98110 → LB 0.98200 (inverted). 2nd: many CV=0.98175 ensembles spread private 0.98130–0.98170. 12th: a 0.0005 public swing is noise, and 5-fold CV on 630K rows beat public as a private predictor. 4th/24th/30th all say final-2 selection, not modeling, decided rank; 30th's rule was "refuse any pick where OOF − public gap is negative".
- **Divergences:** pool size barely predicted rank — 19th reached 19th with 29 models and a Ridge meta; 3rd built 203 models and still missed 1st by a hair; 4th's best GENERATED private (0.98148, not submitted) came from only 9 models, beating his own 0.98200-public 3-way vote (0.98133 private). Where top ranks diverged from mid ranks: 2nd and 5th, who published private scores, landed 0.98151 and 0.98133 while stacking 119–125 L1 sets (1st and 3rd published no private score; 1st's reply puts many of his submissions at private 0.981–0.9815 for the same CV), whereas 12th (41 models) and 30th landed 0.98059–0.98082, and 24th (166 OOF) and 19th published no score at all — a ~0.0005–0.0009 private gap, roughly the width of 2nd's own day-3-to-final decline (0.98160 → 0.98151). The medal order was decided by slot selection and by one decomposition idea (1st's OvR) more than by model count.
- **Highest-leverage single trick:** exploit that the model almost never confuses Low with High — split the 3-class task into two binary classifiers (Low vs Rest, then Medium vs High) and reconstruct probabilities as `P(M)=(1-p1)(1-p2)`, `P(H)=(1-p1)p2` (1st; the winning decomposition, worth beating every 200-model stack). Runner-up trick: the OvR-style middle-vs-outer decomposition reappears in 5th's one special base model.
- **Nothing worked:** pseudo-labeling (12th CV crashed 0.98045 → 0.95390; 24th listed it dead). SMOTE / minority oversampling (24th dead; 12th's LLM reflexively reaches for it and had to forbid it). Greedy forward selection at scale (12th: greedy peaked 7–9 models then degraded and overfit CV, LB 0.97973 vs stacker 0.98082). Correlation pruning when members already correlate >0.995 (12th: −0.0003 CV). Bias/threshold tuning that raises CV but drops private (19th, 24th extreme bias tuning, and 3rd's noisy-LB warning). GraphSAGE GNN as a stack member (3rd and 12th both excluded it — low stacker gain / −0.0001 LB) and YDF (3rd and 24th both discarded it — shifted toward Medium, or "did not integrate well"). TabPFN at 630K rows on free T4 (12th hit the 12h cap on fold 1/5). Reproducing a heavily-shared public notebook as your aggressive slot (30th's 54-team "commodity zone" all collapsed −0.001 on private in lockstep). Line-for-line reproduction across an sklearn version bump (30th: 0.96796 vs the source's 0.98132).
