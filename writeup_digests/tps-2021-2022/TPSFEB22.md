- **TL;DR:** The dataset was generated by `np.random.RandomState(seed).choice(10, size=100, p=...)` with the SAME seed for train and
  test, so train rows and test rows are paired draws, not independent samples.
- He rebuilt the hidden draw sequence from the published 286 decamer counts and classified with a `RadiusNeighborsClassifier` that

- **Architecture:** SINGLE · stages=1 · l1=1 (RadiusNeighborsClassifier) · l2=none · novel=none · mod=none ·
  topo=286 decamer counts per row → inverse-RNG reconstruction back to the ordered 100-decamer draw sequence (hand-built transform)
  → RadiusNeighborsClassifier matching paired train/test rows → class

    sklearn's `RadiusNeighborsClassifier` fit on the transformed training rows — so `no fitted estimator` is the wrong wording here.

    neighbours. What is fitted: the neighbour classifier (radius/membership lookup, no learned coefficients).

- Duplicate structure (the whole exploit): "A third of the training data and a quarter of the test data are duplicates" (within their

- **Features:** the feature engineering IS a data transformation designed to make the paired train/test points visible:
  - Take the 286 decamer counts of a row and convert them back into the ordered list of 100 decamers that was the output of
    `np.random.RandomState.choice()`.
  - Then pick "a data transformation (and a suitable metric) such that data points generated with similar p and the same seed become
    close neighbors".
  - Mechanism, quoted from the numpy source he links: `choice()` first draws the uniform samples and then maps them through
    `cdf = p.cumsum(); idx = cdf.searchsorted(uniform_samples, side='right')` — so changing p moves only the draws that straddle a
    cdf boundary.
  - His worked demonstration: two 30-digit arrays from `RandomState(seed=231)` with `prob_train =
    [0.10,0.10,0.10,0.10,0.1,0.10,0.08,0.12,0.10,0.10]` and `prob_test = [0.12,0.09,0.09,0.15,0.1,0.05,0.10,0.10,0.09,0.11]` differ
    at only three positions (4 vs 3) — same positions, different labels.
  - No encodings, no engineered aggregate columns, no GP features, no dropped columns stated.
- **Models:** `RadiusNeighborsClassifier` on the reconstructed rows; radius, metric and weights: not stated (notebook).
- `ExtraTreesClassifier` is used as the diagnostic that exposes the leak, not as the submitted model.
- Library, seeds, fold counts: not stated.
- **CV:** he insists CV must be computed "correctly (i.e. without duplicated data in the validation set)".
- The key CV-vs-LB observation, qualitative: with correct CV, "ExtraTreesClassifier reaches a public lb score which is substantially

- Splitter, folds, repeats, seeds: not stated.

### TPSFEB22-02 · 2nd · JamieWallis (jwallib) · LB not stated/not stated · CV not stated (no number published)

- **TL;DR:** A voting ensemble of 6 solutions (2 trees, 2 plain KNN metrics, 2 Earth-Mover's-Distance clustering methods), built
  because the rows are histograms and the correct distance for histograms is EMD.
- No accuracy, no CV score, no k, no hyperparameter values published.
- **Architecture:** FLAT · stages=1 · l1=6 (ExtraTrees, LightGBM, KNN-Euclidean, KNN-Manhattan, 2 optimised EMD clustering methods) ·
  l2=none (voting; no meta-learner trained on an OOF matrix) · novel=none · mod=none ·
  topo=[ExtraTrees, LGBM, KNN-Euclid, KNN-Manhattan, EMD-cluster x2] → voting ensemble
  - Rule 1/3: a hard vote over six level-0 members is a blend, not a stack; the vote weights and any soft/hard choice are `not stated`.
  - One member is itself a two-step retrieval funnel — 30 nearest neighbours by Euclidean AND Manhattan, then the closest of those by
    EMD, copy its class — but that happens INSIDE a level-0 member, so the submitted topology stays FLAT rather than CASCADE.
  - Global vs per-series: not applicable (row-level multiclass).
  - He never mentions the train/test leak that decided 1st place, and does not use post-processing: the 6 votes are the whole solution.
- **Setup:** bacteria-species multiclass classification; the class count and the 286-column width are facts from 1st's page, NOT
  stated on this one (rule 4: no carryover) — this page only evidences A/T/G/C 10-mer histogram rows via its own example; rows/cols
  and slot counts not stated.
- Structural quirk he exploits instead of the leak: adjacent k-mers are near-equivalent — "the difference between A10T0G0C0 and
  A9T1G0C0 is much smaller than the difference compared to A0T0G0C10".
- **Features:** none engineered — the provided count columns are consumed as-is by every member (the author calls the data
  "histogram data"; column count not stated on this page). No encodings, no generated features, no dropped columns stated.
- **Models:**
  - ExtraTreesClassifier — "individually optimised by RandomSearchCV" (search space, best params: not stated).
  - LightGBM classifier — also "individually optimised by RandomSearchCV" (params not stated).
  - KNN with Euclidean distance, and KNN with Manhattan distance ("two tuned KNN clustering solutions"; k not stated). Manhattan
    "outperform[ed] the Euclidean KNN when run individually".
  - Two "optimised clustering methods" built on Earth Mover's Distance (Wasserstein): identical species should need less work to shift
    one histogram into another. Cost made the full EMD matrix impossible, so the recipe is: take the 30 closest neighbours by
    Euclidean and the 30 closest by Manhattan, and of those 30 find the closest neighbour by EMD and assign it the same class.
- **CV:** not stated — no splitter, no fold count, no CV number. RandomSearchCV implies CV inside the hyperparameter search only; the

- The 30-neighbour shortlist is acknowledged as an approximation that throws away the metric's full information.

## TPSFEB22 — consensus recipe
- **Architecture distribution:** 1st `SINGLE` (inverse-RNG reconstruction + one RadiusNeighborsClassifier) · 2nd `FLAT` (6-member
  voting ensemble: ExtraTrees, LightGBM, KNN-Euclidean, KNN-Manhattan, 2 EMD clustering members).
  - **winner topology: `SINGLE`** — 1st submitted one neighbour classifier over a hand-built transform; 2nd submitted a six-way vote
    of conventional members. The order is decided by where they looked, not by how much they blended.
  - Neither entry carries a `STACK2`/`CASCADE` path, neither uses community OOF/prediction files (`mod=public-oof` appears nowhere),
    and neither publishes a single accuracy number, so no score comparison inside this set is possible.
- **New architectures at the board:** none — `novel=none` on both entries. Every estimator named on either page is stock sklearn or
  LightGBM (`RadiusNeighborsClassifier`, `ExtraTreesClassifier`, KNN, `LGBMClassifier`); no neural net, no tabular transformer and no
  AutoML package appears anywhere in this two-entry set.
  - The non-stock ingredients are a data transform and a distance metric, not architectures: 1st's inverse-RNG reconstruction of the
    100-decamer draw sequence, and 2nd's Earth Mover's Distance (Wasserstein) nearest-neighbour member, itself a 30-candidate funnel.
- **Agreed on (2 of 2 — TPSFEB22-01, TPSFEB22-02):** replace the metric before adding model capacity. 1st searches for "a data
  transformation (and a suitable metric) such that data points generated with similar p and the same seed become close neighbors";
  2nd moves from Euclidean to Manhattan to EMD because "the data consisted histogram data". AmbrosM confirms the shared logic from
  1st's comment on 2nd's page: "both aimed at finding a better metric than the Manhattan distance: You found EMD, I transformed the
  data and got something similar to a 1-d EMD."
- **Agreed on (2 of 2 — TPSFEB22-01, TPSFEB22-02):** instance/neighbour-based classification as the final mechanism — 1st submits a
  RadiusNeighborsClassifier; 4 of 2nd's 6 members are neighbour retrieval (KNN-Euclidean, KNN-Manhattan, and the two EMD members,
  each of which assigns "the closest neighbour ... and the same class").
- **Agreed on (2 of 2 — TPSFEB22-01, TPSFEB22-02):** `ExtraTreesClassifier` appears in both pipelines, but for opposite reasons — 1st
  uses it as the leak detector ("public lb score > cv score"), 2nd uses it as one of two tree members in the vote.
- **Divergences:** the entire gap is diagnosis, not topology. 1st found the generation bug (same seed, `p.cumsum()` searchsorted) and
  turned each test row into a search for its train twin; 2nd never mentions the leak and instead approximates the right distance
  because the exact EMD matrix was computationally infeasible.
- 2nd's members are individually hyperparameter-searched (RandomSearchCV on both tree models, "tuned" KNN) while 1st publishes no
  tuning at all — capacity search versus structural insight, and the structural insight placed higher.
- Post-processing diverges by omission: 1st documents that the distribution shift sent "many competition participants" to post-process
  their probabilities, and replaces all of it; 2nd states no post-processing step either way.
- **Highest-leverage single trick:** read the generator. `np.random.RandomState.choice()` draws uniforms once and maps them through
  `p.cumsum()`, so the same seed with slightly different p produces near-identical rows — reconstructing the draw order makes train
  rows match their test twins, and the metric does the rest (TPSFEB22-01; corroborated in the comment on TPSFEB22-02 where 2nd's own
  EMD is described by 1st as "something similar to a 1-d EMD").
- **Nothing worked:** exact all-pairs Earth Mover's Distance — "it wasn't feasible to work out the EMD between all samples", forcing the
  30-neighbour Euclidean+Manhattan shortlist (TPSFEB22-02).
- **Nothing worked:** Euclidean as the primary metric for count histograms — Manhattan beat it when run individually (TPSFEB22-02).
- **Nothing worked:** KL divergence as a distance for this data — proposed by Jonathan McKinney (15th), answered by 2nd as appropriate
  for continuous rather than binned histogram data (TPSFEB22-02 comments).
- **Nothing worked:** reverse-engineering the train/test drift as a resampling bias — Samuel Cortinhas (285th): "I tried to reverse
  engineer the train - test drift by resampling from the bias but it turns out it was due to something so much more simple"
  (TPSFEB22-01 comments).
