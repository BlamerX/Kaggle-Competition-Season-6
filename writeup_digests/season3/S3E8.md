### S3E8-08 · 8th · Sergey Saharovskiy (sergiosaharovskiy / SSS) · LB not stated/not stated · CV not stated

- **Link:** https://www.kaggle.com/c/playground-series-s3e8/discussion/392860

- **TL;DR:** The medal came from deterministic gemological CLIP rules on top of an ensemble, not from a new model — per (carat × cut × clarity × color) groups he clipped test predictions to the train Q3+1.5·IQR upper bound (+0.2) then forced them up to the train min (+1.1), flying his public LB to #1 ("flying over the 1st place again").
- **Architecture:** FLAT · stages=1 · l1=an ensemble ("best ensembled submission") — composition NOT stated in this writeup · l2=none ·
  novel=none · mod=none ·
  topo=[ensemble of regression models, members not stated] → deterministic per-group upper-bound clip (Q3+1.5·IQR ×alpha) → deterministic per-group lower-bound assign (min train price) → submission
  - The fitted model is only the base; the reported rank was earned entirely by the rule-based post-processing (ruling 2), which is a group-wise clipping formula with no extra fitted estimator.
- **Setup:** gemstone RMSE; base ensemble compared against public XGB/LGB/CatBoost ensembles he found "good and generalized well from the very beginning"; references the original data as `cubic_zirconia.csv` (train vs original differ in the outlier regions). Rows/cols/slots not stated. Structural quirk exploited: the synthetic train/test share reproducible gem-quality price bands, so predictions can be bounded by group statistics.
- **Features:** not stated (post-processing operates on the finished submission, not features).
- **Models:** the base "best ensembled submission"; the specific models/params are NOT published in this writeup (he only points to tetsutani's and eamonntweedy's similar modeling public notebooks). Source silent on the ensemble's composition.
- **CV:** clipping the *train* outliers (Part 1) gave an "extreme boost on CV but poor results on LB" — because the models already squashed the outliers internally; the winning clips are judged on LB, not CV.
- **Ensembling:** he takes his best ensembled submission as input (`best_sub`); the ensemble weights/composition not stated. Shared-OOF usage: not stated.
- **Post-processing:** THE whole solution, and it is where his gains live (ruling 2 = post-processing, not a stage):
  - Upper-bound clip: bin carat `np.arange(0.19, 4.5, 0.035)`; for each (cut in [Ideal,Premium], clarity, color in [D,E]) train group with >5 rows compute Q1/Q3/IQR and clip the matching test predictions to [0, upper_bound × alpha]; alpha tunable.
  - Lower-bound assign: slide a 0.15-wide carat window over ['Ideal','Premium','Very Good','Good','Fair'] × clarity ['IF','VVS1','VVS2','VS1','VS2','SI1','SI2','I1'] × color groups [['D','E'],['F','G'],['H','I'],['J']]; where a test group's min prediction < the train group's min price (and train>4 / test>3 rows), assign the train min. Wrapped in joblib `Parallel(n_jobs=-1)` over `np.arange(0.20, 5, 0.15)`.
- **Gains:** (LB +0.011, 574.92723→574.9163) 20-example manual upper-bound clip; (LB +0.2) automated upper-bound clip with tuned alpha; (LB +1.1) lower-bound assign — "pushing it straight to the Top 10" and #1 on public LB. (These are PUBLIC LB gains, not CV.)

## S3E8 — consensus recipe
- **Architecture distribution:** 2nd `STACK2` (60 L1 → Ridge meta on OOF) · 3rd `FLAT` (XGB/LGBM/CatBoost over 3 selection datasets, 10-seed avg) · 6th `AUTOML` (AutoGluon 8-fold stacks + AutoXGB, CV-weighted) · 7th `FLAT` (median of 17, including reverse + double-back predictions-as-features members) · 8th `FLAT` (base ensemble, composition unstated, + deterministic per-group clip post-processing).
  - **winner topology: `STACK2`** — 2nd is the only true two-level stack (Ridge fitted on the OOF matrix) and it took the best rank among these writeups; the flat/multi-view blends (3rd/7th/8th) and the vendor stack (6th) spread across 3rd–8th.
- **New architectures at the board:** the only non-stock system is `AutoGluon` (6th — 8-fold level-1 stack inferring LGBM/CatBoost/XGBoost/RandomForest/NNTorch/NNFastAI + a pseudo-label variant), and it medalled at 6th.
  - Neural nets appear only as *level-2 blenders* at 2nd (slightly better CV than Ridge, then a public/private rebound) and as AutoGluon internals at 6th; no novel *tabular transformer* family is reported.
  - 7th's reversed/double-back pipeline is a novel *workflow*, not a novel model — its chassis are stock public notebooks (mostly XGB/LGBM/CatBoost ensembles, plus one NN chassis, pawebiegun's).
- **Agreed on (4 of 5 — S3E8-02, S3E8-03, S3E8-06, S3E8-07):** XGBoost + LightGBM + CatBoost are the base family everywhere (3rd builds the whole solution on them, 6th's AutoGluon/AutoXGB infer those same trees, most of 7th's chassis notebooks are XGB/LGB/Cat ensembles (one NN chassis), 2nd's 60 L1 models are XGB/LGBM/CatBoost + some Ridge). 8th references them only via others' public notebooks, so his own composition is unstated.
- **Agreed on (2 of 5 — S3E8-02, S3E8-06):** using BOTH the original and generated data in training. 2nd: original-dataset use is worth about −1.6 CV. 6th: "Both the datasets where used, original and generated." NOTE the split is not unanimous — 7th had to exclude the original data from his double-back models (no reversed predictions available for it), and 3rd never states it.
- **Agreed on (2 of 5 — S3E8-06, S3E8-07):** lean on the public notebook ecosystem — community models are load-bearing INSIDE the submission: 7th's entire 17-model median and 6th's AutoGluon/AutoXGB stack are built from public notebooks. 2nd credits nothing external (everything came from his own framework), and 3rd/8th cite public notebooks only as inspiration, not as submitted members.
- **Divergences:** the top of the board is a modelling race (2nd's Ridge-on-OOF stack, 3rd's std-controlled multi-view blend) while the middle buys rank with systems (6th AutoGluon) or tricks (7th reversal, 8th clipping). 8th reached 8th almost purely through post-hoc group clipping (+0.2 then +1.1 public) with no published base model, whereas 2nd published 1,816 models. Score gap that IS measurable: 7th's final 570.53 private is the only concrete number, and it beat 2nd's stated L1 cutoff (572.6) on private, hinting the median-of-17 + shake-up outscored the honest stack on the leaderboard despite 2nd ranking above it in this set.
- **Highest-leverage single trick:** deterministic per-group prediction bounding on the synthetic price bands — 8th clips test predictions to train Q3+1.5·IQR (upper) and up to the train group min (lower), for +0.2 and +1.1 public LB and a #1-public jump (S3E8-08). Its caveat: gains are public-only and the analogous CV-side outlier fix backfired.
- **Nothing worked:** exploiting the train/original → test duplicates by overlaying known prices — 2nd measured public −0.05 but private +0.1, so he submitted WITHOUT the exploit and it stayed the right call (S3E8-02).
- **Nothing worked:** level-2 blending and level-2 neural nets at 2nd — L2 blends underperformed Ridge, and all L2 NNs rebounded hard on public LB (confirmed overfit on private).
- **Nothing worked:** fixing/clipping the *training* outliers (8th Part 1): huge CV lift, worse LB, because the GBMs already squashed outliers — and independently echoed by a 32nd-place commenter.
- **Nothing worked:** chasing tiny RMSE wins with high fold-std (3rd): a −RMSE feature drop whose fold std rose was discarded, which is why he built a std-only-optimisation dataset.
