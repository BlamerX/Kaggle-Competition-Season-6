- **TL;DR:** XGBoost + LightGBM + a PLE-encoded neural net, combined by a second neural net that learns one weight per
  class per model from all three inputs.
- His thesis for the episode: "I had trouble getting good results with them [NNs] in past competitions, often finding
  they performed worse in the private leaderboard ... this time I think they were the secret sauce".
- **Architecture:** STACK2 · stages=2 · l1=3 prediction streams (XGBoost, LightGBM, PLE-MLP) built from 10 Optuna
  hyperparameter sets for the GBDTs · l2=neural-network stacker trained on the three level-1 prediction sets (the
  author calls it "stacking"; he never states whether the meta trains on OOF vs held labels — see Comments) ·
  novel=MLP+embedding · mod=none ·
  topo=[XGBoost avg(10 Optuna sets) + LightGBM avg(10 sets) + PLE-MLP] → NN stacker (per-class weights conditioned on all 3 inputs) → submission
  - Level 2 is a **trained NN meta-learner consuming the three level-1 prediction sets** (stacking), not a
    hand/Optuna-weighted average, so this is a genuine `STACK2` and not `FLAT` (contrast with ruling 1). The author's
    PLE/embedding layers live **inside** the level-0 NN, whose *prediction* is a stack input; they are never fed as
    features to a downstream GBM, so `mod=embed`/`CASCADE` (ruling 5) does not apply.
  - The submission was the stack, not one member — though the PLE-MLP alone already scored ~0.401 on the private LB
    (top 10%), so he could have submitted it.
  - The stacker differs from a plain average in two specific ways: each class probability of each input gets its own
    weight (not one weight per model), and each input's weights are computed from **all three** predictions rather than
    only from itself; that pair of changes was worth 0.004 log loss against a simple average.
  - The internal 10-set averaging of XGBoost/LightGBM is seed/hyperparameter averaging inside one architecture (ruling 4)
    and adds no level.
