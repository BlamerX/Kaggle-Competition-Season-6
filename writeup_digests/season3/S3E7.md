### S3E7-01 · 1st · hardyxu52 (Hardy Xu) · LB not stated/not stated · CV not stated (3-fold x 3-repeat, no absolute value published)

- **Link:** https://www.kaggle.com/c/playground-series-s3e7/discussion/390976
- **Status:** FETCHED (1 attempt; `/c/` discussion form returned 11,224 B incl. 15 comments)
- **TL;DR:** Plain uniform average of 4 XGB + 2 LGBM submissions, no stacking, no meta-learner.
- The win came from exploiting the duplicate structure after predicting (+.014 LB from inverting the 716 cross-set dups; the author says "leaderboard score", not explicitly public).
- **Architecture:** FLAT · stages=1 · l1=6 (4 XGB + 2 LGBM) · l2=none (uniform average) · novel=none · mod=none ·
  topo=6 XGB/LGBM configs (categorical handling x reduced-original-dataset) -> per-fold avg -> uniform avg of 6
  - Level 0 = two GBM families in 6 feature/dataset variants, blended with equal weights and no fitted blender.
  - Fold averaging sits inside each config (predictions from each of the 3 folds averaged), not a second level.
- **Setup:** 1,531 duplicate pairs after dropping booking_status -> 562 train pairs (opposite labels), 253 test pairs, 716 train+test pairs; positive/base rate ~38%.
  Structural quirk exploited: the 716 train<->test dup pairs and the 253 test-test dup pairs.
- **Features:** type_of_meal_plan, room_type_reserved, market_segment_type identified as categorical and handled 3 ways (as-is; one-hot; LGBM native categorical).
  Two data arms: full original dataset vs reduced (drop ~17% that adversarial validation flagged as least train-like, from a bimodal predicted-membership distribution).
  "Didn't find anything that improved my CV significantly" for any additional engineered feature.
- **Models:** XGBoost `exact` tree method (not `hist`) with max_depth ~12-13; LightGBM with larger max_leaves and max_bin — "increased tree expressiveness" was the tuning key.
  6 winning variants: XGB; XGB+OH-cat; XGB+reduced-orig; XGB+OH-cat+reduced-orig; LGBM+reduced-orig; LGBM+cat+reduced-orig. Search ranges, seeds, versions: not stated.
- **CV:** stratified 3-fold x 3 repeats; fold predictions averaged; built a holdout set to test the duplicate-removal. No absolute CV value published.
- **Ensembling:** uniform (unweighted) average of the 4 XGB + 2 LGBM submissions; 3-fold averaging within each. No hill-climb, no weights, no meta-learner.
- **Post-processing:** (a) 716 test rows whose train twin exists -> set prediction to the opposite of the twin -> +.014 (LB; author says "boost of +.014 to my leaderboard score"); (b) 253 test-test duplicate pairs -> set to 0.5
  (credited @siukeitin) -> +.003 (author's score, pub/priv not stated); (c) removed the 562 train dup pairs + the 716 train rows with a test twin from training (minor private gain, made CV more reliable).
- **Gains:** (+0.014 AUC, LB per author) invert the 716 cross-set duplicates. (+0.003 AUC) predict 0.5 for the 253 test-test dup pairs. (minor private, magnitude not stated)
  removing train duplicates. Tree-expressiveness tuning (exact / max_depth 12-13 / larger LGBM max_bin) beat his usual ranges (delta not stated).
- **Failed:** extra engineered features — "didn't find anything that improved my CV significantly". Fixing dates — no significant CV improvement. Predicting mean or median
  for the 253 dup pairs instead of 0.5 — both decreased the score vs 0.5.
- **Comments:** 15 comments mined — @siukeitin credited with the 0.5-for-253-dup-pairs trick; community independently found the 716-record leak (discussion 388851).
  Jose Caliz (2nd) argues 0.5 is standard but the median/percentile is theoretically better for AUC; Hardy replies he tried mean and median and both scored below 0.5, and
  questions the median because train base rate ~38% while the dup pairs are 50/50 (so he'd predict above the median).

### S3E7-02 · 2nd · jcaliz (Jose Caliz) · LB not stated/not stated · CV not stated (20/30-fold by booking-date, "perfectly correlated" with LB)

- **Link:** https://www.kaggle.com/c/playground-series-s3e7/discussion/390956
- **Status:** FETCHED (1 attempt; 7,541 B incl. 8 comments)
- **TL;DR:** 16-model GBM pool selected by hill-climb with Nelder-Mead weights (not ranked).
- Exploits BOTH duplicate leaks: opposite-label for cross-set dups, and a 62nd-percentile overwrite for test-test dups.
- **Architecture:** FLAT · stages=1 · l1=16 (mostly LGBM + a couple XGBoost) · l2=none (hill-climb selection + Nelder-Mead weights) · novel=none · mod=none (author did NOT rank) ·
  topo=LGBM/XGB x{20- or 30-fold, orig data per fold} -> hill-climb select 16 -> Nelder-Mead weights
  - 16 members chosen greedily by hill-climbing and combined with Nelder-Mead-optimised weights on raw (un-ranked) probabilities.
  - Per Rule 1 / ruling 1, weight selection over an OOF pool is `FLAT`, not `STACK2` — no meta-learner was fitted on the OOF matrix.
- **Setup:** duplicates always come in opposite-label pairs; positive rate ~38%. Original dataset concatenated into every training fold (a 20-fold CV = entire original + 95% of train per fold).
- **Features:** mostly out-of-box; added `is_generated` and `is_inconsistent` indicators; `Dayofyear` kept in some models via Sequential Feature Selection.
- **Models:** LGBM (majority) + XGBoost (a couple); all trained with EarlyStopping; test predictions = mean over each fold-model. 16-model pool; exact hyperparameters not stated.
- **CV:** stratified KFold, k=20 (cheaper) or 30 (expensive), stratified on booking-date rather than target (some months were otherwise all in one fold). Perfect CV-LB correlation. Absolute CV not published.
- **Ensembling:** Hill-Climb member selection + Nelder-Mead weight optimisation over the 16 members; explicitly no rank-blend; weights applied to raw predictions.
- **Post-processing:** (a) test dups that appear in train -> assign the opposite of the known label; (b) test-test dups -> overwrite with the 62nd-percentile of predictions (best AUC point
  for a 50/50 pair given ~38% positive rate, because un-calibrated tree probabilities sit near 0/1).
- **Gains:** the two duplicate tricks are the stated differentiators (no absolute deltas published); the 62nd-percentile-over-0.5 comparison is a screenshot showing the 62nd-percentile submission scored better on public LB. The hill-climb 16-model + Nelder-Mead blend is the submission.
- **Failed:** Holidays features; day-of-month feature; an "inconsistent day" indicator; more than 30 folds; deliberately did NOT test the other community-found datasets (dataset big enough, wanted fast iteration).
- **Comments:** 8 comments mined — asked to explain hill-climb / Nelder-Mead (not answered technically); Pasquale asks 0.5 vs the 0.62 percentile — Jose replies 0.5 is only optimal if well-calibrated and tree models aren't on imbalanced data, and posts a screenshot (green = 62nd, red = 0.5) preferring the 62nd-percentile submission; on other datasets he declined to keep iteration fast.
- **Compute:** 20/30-fold on original+train concat — "computation was expensive" (why 20-fold used); absolute wall-clock/GPU not stated.
- **Artifacts:**
  - https://www.kaggle.com/jcaliz (author)
  - https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/playground-series-s3e7/writeups/jose-c-liz-2nd-place-solution (citation)

## S3E7 — consensus recipe
- **Architecture distribution:** 1st `S3E7-01` FLAT (uniform avg of 6 XGB/LGBM) · 2nd `S3E7-02` FLAT (16 models, hill-climb + Nelder-Mead) ·
  3rd `S3E7-03` STACK2 (TF-NN meta over 123 OOF cols) · 4th `S3E7-04` FLAT (3 GBMs, scipy per-fold weights) · 9th `S3E7-09` FLAT (7 XGB, hill-climb) ·
  15th `S3E7-15` FLAT (7 models, per-fold "Averager").
  - **winner topology: FLAT** — 1st place submitted an unweighted average; the lone STACK2 finished 3rd; 5 of 6 entrants were FLAT blends and none of the weight searches qualifies as STACK2 (rule 1 / ruling 1).
- **New architectures at the board:** **none.** Every entrant ran stock XGBoost / LightGBM / CatBoost (plus 3rd's TensorFlow NN and Ridge as L2 *meta-learners* — a plain MLP meta, not a novel tabular architecture).
  No 2023-era novel tabular net (TabM / FT-Transformer / NODE / Trompt / RealMLP / GANDALF / DANet) appears in any entry.
- **Agreed on (5 of 6 — S3E7-01, -02, -03, -04, -09) — the core lever:** the duplicate-leak post-processing. 1st and 2nd exploit both leaks themselves; 3rd/4th/9th apply the public 388992 inversion snippet. 15th (`S3E7-15`) never mentions it.
- **Agreed on (6 of 6):** tree models are the ONLY family — every level-0 model is XGBoost, LightGBM, or CatBoost (1st/2nd use XGB+LGBM without CatBoost; 9th discards LGBM/CatBoost down to XGB-only), except that 15th's pool also carries one Random Forest member; no NN or linear model appears at level 0.
- **Agreed on (5 of 6 — S3E7-01, -02, -03, -04, -15):** minimal / zero feature engineering. 1st, 3rd, 4th and 15th explicitly found FE unhelpful or used none (3rd measured -0.004 from time features); 2nd kept only 3 indicator/date columns.
- **Agreed on (3 of 6 — S3E7-02, -03, -04):** CV tracked the LB well this season — 2nd "perfectly correlated", 3rd "metrics and LB in good agreement" after ~10 submissions, 4th "relatively stable correlation between cv and lb".
- **Agreed on (3 of 6 — S3E7-01, -02, -03):** fold the original/source dataset into training — 1st ran reduced vs full original arms, 2nd concatenated the original into every fold, 3rd mixed it in and scored CV on competition-only rows (+0.002).
- **Divergences:** the only STACK2 (3rd: NN meta +0.0026 CV over best single) finished behind two FLAT blends that leaned harder on the +0.0141-public duplicate inversion; 9th went XGB-only (discarding LGBM/CatBoost) while 4th and 15th kept a mixed GBM pool — no author publishes absolute AUC, so the exact 1st-vs-3rd gap is unrecoverable from these pages.
- **Highest-leverage single trick:** the duplicate-leak label flips — 1st's inversion of the 716 cross-set duplicates (+.014 on the LB, "leaderboard score" per author) and 0.5-for-253-test-pairs (+.003), echoed by 3rd (+0.0141 public) and 9th (0.5-for-253 would be top-1 public / top-2 private). Source `S3E7-01`, `S3E7-03`, `S3E7-09`.
- **Nothing worked:** feature engineering generally — time-based features cost 3rd -0.004 CV; 1st/4th/15th report little or no FE benefit; pseudo-labeling (4th) inflated CV but hurt LB sharply (overfit). For 9th LGBM and CatBoost yielded worse CV-vs-LB scores than XGB and were discarded.
