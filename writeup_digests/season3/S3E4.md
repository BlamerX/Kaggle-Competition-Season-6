### S3E4-30 · 30th · Hardy Xu (hardyxu52) · LB not stated (`.8333` cited as the no-original-data hypothetical) · CV not stated (TimeSeriesSplit / 5-fold x 10-repeat StratifiedKFold)

- **Link:** https://www.kaggle.com/c/playground-series-s3e4/discussion/382447
- **Status:** FETCHED (jina `/c/` markdown form; 5 Comments incl. author-relevant replies in the dump)
- **TL;DR:** 50 same-config XGBoost models averaged (StratifiedKFold 5x10), time-aware CV; two FE/rule tweaks gave measurable AUC gains, and merging original fraud data cost him 1st place.
- **Architecture:** SEED · stages=1 · l1=50 XGBoost models, identical hyperparameters, trained across 5-fold x 10-repeat StratifiedKFold · l2=none · novel=none · mod=none ·
  topo=V-feature daily-mean subtraction + merged original fraud → 50 same-HP XGBoost (StratifiedKFold 5x10) → average → V14/V21 pure-group override → submitted
  - Multi-fold/multi-seed averaging of a single architecture = `SEED` (Rule 4 / Ruling 4: fold and seed averaging inside one family never counts as a level).
  - The V14+V21 "predict 0" rule is applied after `predict_proba`, so it is Post-processing (Ruling 2), not a stage.
- **Setup:** train/test are split by time (a `Time` component), which drove the CV choice; used the entire competition train set PLUS the fraud instances from the original Credit Card Fraud data. Row/col counts not stated.
- **Features:**
  - Transformed versions of the V features by subtracting the daily average for that feature (per paddykb's day-1-vs-day-2 observation). Author's exact framing: features trend up/down within a day similarly between days, but shifted vertically — subtracting the daily mean uncovers signal.
  - (No other engineered columns named; base `V1..V28`, `Amount`, `Time` are the dataset's own.)
- **Models:** XGBoost — 50 identical-hyperparameter copies. Per-model hyperparameters, learning rate, depth, seeds: source silent (he says "the same hyperparameters", values not published). Library version not stated.
- **CV:** sklearn `TimeSeriesSplit` used for hyperparameter optimization ("at the very least it seemed fairly reliable"); model training/validation via Stratified KFold, 5 folds x 10 repeats. No CV AUC number published; CV-vs-LB gap not quantified (he was unsure of the time-split CV's reliability).
  - Trust verdict: wary — "made me more unsure of how to best build a local CV schema and unsure of its reliability."
- **Ensembling:** plain arithmetic average of the 50 XGBoost fold predictions; no weights, no meta-learner, no hill-climb. Shared-OOF usage: none stated (uses the original dataset as extra rows, not as prediction files).
- **Post-processing:** deterministic override — group train by V feature pairs, found `V14 x V21` yields pure fraud/non-fraud groups; 466 test rows whose (V14,V21) pair also exists in train were force-predicted `0`. Applied after the model's probabilities (Ruling 2).
- **Gains:**
  - (+.0015 AUC Private, 0 Public) subtracting the daily average from each V feature (did not move Public, boosted Private).
  - (+.0004 AUC, both Public and Private) the V14 x V21 pure-group override on 466 test rows.
- **Failed:** (the author's stated self-diagnosis)
  - Including the original fraud data cost him the win: "Had I excluded the original data, I would've scored .8333, enough for 1st place ... my instinct was telling me to not use the original data, but it was hard to go against what my CV and the Public LB were telling me."
  - TimeSeriesSplit CV was used but its benefit was questioned by a peer (see Comments), and Hardy himself flags uncertainty in the time-split CV.
- **Comments:** mined; two replies carry content:
  - Matt OP (54th) concurs it was the hardest Playground of the month and reveals he used a *filtered* original set instead: `original.query("Class == 1 and Time > 120580")`.
  - Oscar Aguilar (76th): "I also tried TimeSeriesSplit for CV but it didn't help in the LB score." (peer counter-evidence to Hardy's time-CV).
  - snats (487th) asks for ensemble resources; Minh and Adeel congratulate — no further numbers.
- **Compute:** 50 XGBoost models x 5-fold x 10 repeats (a large fit-count); wall-clock, GPU/CPU, RAM not stated.
- **Artifacts:** (verbatim, not fetched)
  - https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/playground-series-s3e4/discussion/381415#2116636 (paddykb comment on day-1 vs day-2 feature behavior)
  - https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/playground-series-s3e4/discussion/381483#2117170 (siukeitin comment on V features identifying customers)
  - https://www.kaggle.com/paddykb ; https://www.kaggle.com/siukeitin (commenter profiles referenced)

- **Lesson:** On a time-split fraud set, subtract the per-day mean from V features and force-predict pure (V14,V21) groups — but never merge the original fraud data here: it aligned with CV yet cost 1st place.

## S3E4 — consensus recipe
- **Architecture distribution:** 10th `SINGLE` (one Optuna-tuned CatBoost with focal loss, 10-fold averaged) · 30th `SEED` (50 identical XGBoost averaged, 5-fold x 10-repeat, plus a post-hoc override) · 36th `FLAT` (Optuna-weighted ensemble, per-model permutation-importance feature dropping).
  - **winner-of-file topology: `SINGLE`** — the best-placing entry here (10th) submitted one GBM; the wider ensembles finished 30th and 36th.
  - Read-across: this trio is a clean natural experiment — a single tuned focal-loss CatBoost out-blended both a 50-model seed average and an Optuna ensemble, i.e. ensembling width bought nothing on this leaderboard.
- **New architectures at the board:** none — every named model is a stock 2023 GBM (CatBoost, XGBoost); the only non-stock element is a custom focal-loss objective on 10th's CatBoost (a loss, not an architecture).
- **Agreed on (3 of 3 — S3E4-10, S3E4-30, S3E4-36): merge the original Credit Card Fraud dataset with the competition data.** 10th "used both competition and original datasets"; 30th "entire train dataset plus the instances of fraud from the original"; 36th "added the original data". Critical nuance: 30th reports this merge *cost him the win* (.8333 achievable without it), and Matt OP (commenter) hedged by filtering to `original.query("Class == 1 and Time > 120580")` — so the community split on how much original data to add.
- **Agreed on (2 of 3 — S3E4-10, S3E4-36): Optuna.** 10th tunes CatBoost hyperparameters with Optuna TPE (~50 trials); 36th uses Optuna to find ensemble weight proportions. 30th uses no Optuna (fixed XGBoost hyperparameters).
- **Divergences:** rank order did not follow ensemble size — 10th (single model) > 30th (50 models) > 36th (Optuna-weighted blend); the 20-place spread between them came from the original-data decision and FE, not from the blender. 36th's per-model feature dropping (multi-view) is a step no one else took; 30th's time-aware CV and V14/V21 override are his alone.
- **Highest-leverage single trick:** (S3E4-30) subtract the per-day mean from the anonymized V features — worth +.0015 AUC on the Private LB (with zero Public movement), the largest quantified gain in the set.
- **Nothing worked:** adding the *full* original fraud data (30th's own post-mortem: it cost him 1st place, ".8333 achievable without it"). Peer-reported: TimeSeriesSplit CV did not translate to LB gains for 76th (Oscar Aguilar's comment on the 30th page) — but 30th himself still found it "fairly reliable" for hyperparameter tuning, so it is not a shared dead end. No entrant published a quantified failed-model or failed-feature ablation.
