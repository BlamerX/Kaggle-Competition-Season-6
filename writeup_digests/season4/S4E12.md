### S4E12-01 · 1st · cdeotte (Chris Deotte) · LB not stated/not stated · CV 1.016 (full) / 1.019 (published simple version)

- **Link:** https://www.kaggle.com/c/playground-series-s4e12/discussion/554328
- **Status:** FETCHED (first try on the `/c/` discussion form + `X-Return-Format: markdown`; 22 KB body + all 95 comments)
- **TL;DR:** Won with ONE XGBoost model on 611 hand-engineered features, no ensembling.
- Feature engineering moved both CV and LB here, unlike his two prior playgrounds, so he abandoned the large-diverse-ensemble plan for a single model.
- Categorical combination search ran for days on GPU cuDF-Pandas to pick the features.
- **Architecture:** SINGLE · stages=1 · l1=1 (one XGBoost) · l2=none · novel=none · mod=none ·
  topo=GPU random search over 145k column-combos -> best 20 combos + 7 inputs per categorical (the original plus 6 of the encodings the
  author names: label / TE mean / TE median / TE min / TE max / TE nunique / CE) -> 611 features -> single XGBoost
  - The submitted prediction is one fitted estimator (XGBoost) on the engineered matrix; there is no blend,
    no meta-learner, no second family.
  - Internal fold structure only appears inside `target_encode(kfold=...)`; that is a feature transform, not a level.
  - This is the counter-example at the board: 1st used a single model while 2nd and 9th both stacked.
- **Setup:** Train data has 1.2 million rows (author comment). After decomposing `Policy Start Date` into year/month/day/hour/etc
  there are 23 original columns; combinations of 2-6 columns create a candidate pool of 145,000 new columns.
- Final model uses 611 features; the published simple version uses 229 of them.
- Submission slots: not stated. Structural quirk exploited: high-cardinality categoricals gain most from multiple encodings
  plus cross-column combinations.
- **Features:** Per categorical column the author feeds "the original column plus 6 different representations ... All 7 of these
  different encodings are input to the model"; the encodings he names are label, `TE mean` + `TE median`
  + `TE min` + `TE max` + `TE nunique` + `CE` (count encoding). (Author's stated preference, comment reply: `TE_mean` and `CE` are
  usually best; add `TE_median/min/max/nunique` after that.)
- New categorical columns built by combining existing columns with `train['new'] = train.ColA + "_" + train.ColB` (and 3-,4-,5-,6-way);
  example `Occupation`(3) x `Gender`(2) -> 6-value column `Self-Employed_Female`, etc.
- Numerical columns are also treated as categorical and given the same `TE`/`CE` encodings.
- GPU cuDF-Pandas random for-loop over thousands of random combinations; keeps any combination whose TE/CE improves CV; found 170 useful
  combinations over multiple days; publishes the best 20.
- Feature-source: none borrowed; all self-generated. Columns dropped: not stated.
- **Models:** XGBoost (single). Full model: `learning_rate = 0.001`, `n_estimators = 20_000`, `target_encode(kfold=10)`, 611 features.
- Published simple version: `learning_rate = 0.01`, `n_estimators = 2_000`, `target_encode(kfold=5)`, 229 of 611 features.
- Seeds, max_depth, and other XGBoost hyperparameters: not stated. Library versions: not stated.
- **CV:** Splitter not named; `target_encode` uses 10-fold (full) or 5-fold (simple); #repeats and seeds not stated.
- CV RMSLE: full model 1.016; simple published version 1.019.
- CV-vs-LB gap: not stated (author publishes no LB figure in the body).
- Author's trust verdict: on this dataset FE improved CV and LB together (comment: 1.2M rows make chance CV gains unlikely).
- **Ensembling:** None - one XGBoost. Shared-OOF usage: none.
- **Post-processing:** Not stated. (The one deliberate encoding shortcut: in `target_encode` the smoothing mean `mn` is computed on the
  full `train` df rather than the per-fold `df_tmp`, which the author acknowledges is mildly leaky but helps - see Comments.)
- **Gains (RMSLE, lower better):** (-0.003 CV) full model 1.019 -> 1.016 by going 229 -> 611 features, LR 0.01 -> 0.001,
  n_estimators 2,000 -> 20,000, TE kfold 5 -> 10.
- (unquantified) each categorical encoding family and each accepted combination column improved CV and LB; no per-feature delta published.
- **Failed:** No failed feature, model, or blender is named in this writeup (checked full body + 95 comments).
- Anti-knowledge of record: his prior two playgrounds (Sept 2024, Nov 2024) - there FE did not move CV/LB, so he built large diverse
  ensembles instead; the win here came from FE paying off, allowing a single model.
- **Comments:** author replies carry the loadable detail (mined above):
  - Overfitting risk at 145k combos: 1.2M-row train makes it "hard to get lucky with 1.2 million OOF predictions all being better by chance";
    with less data restrict to combinations of 2 and 3, not 2-6.
  - TE leakage: using `train` instead of `df_tmp` per fold is leaky-but-better because every NaN maps to one mean; per-fold means turn NaN
    into 5 different values so the GBDT works harder to predict them identically.
  - Best encodings: `TE_mean` + `CE` usually top, then `TE_median/min/max/nunique`.
  - GPU value (stated in the body, not a reply): cuDF-Pandas lets him "search 10x to 100x more combinations than using CPU".
- **Compute:** Full model 6 hours on 1x A100 GPU (feature engineer + train). Simple version 2 hours on 1x T4 GPU. The combination search
  ran a GPU for-loop "day and night" for multiple days.
- **Artifacts:** (cited by the author, verbatim; not fetched)
  - https://www.kaggle.com/code/cdeotte/first-place-single-model-cv-1-016-lb-1-016
  - https://www.kaggle.com/code/cdeotte/rapids-cuml-lasso-lb-0-72500-cv-0-72800 (the September-playground combination-20-column idea)
  - https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/playground-series-s4e12/writeups/chris-deotte-1st-place-single-model-feature-engine (citation line)
  - https://rapids.ai/cudf-pandas/
  - https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai-data-science/kaggle-grandmasters/ (KGMON workshop)
  - https://www.nvidia.com/gtc/
  - https://www.kaggle.com/cdeotte
- **Lesson:** When target/count encoding plus cross-column combinations actually move CV, a single heavily-engineered XGBoost can beat a
  118-model stack - spend the last week on features, not the blender.

### S4E12-02 · 2nd · SCRIPTCHEF (noodl35) · LB not stated/not stated · CV not stated (early CV 1.031 -> 1.027 with original data)

- **Link:** https://www.kaggle.com/c/playground-series-s4e12/discussion/554505
- **Status:** FETCHED (first try on the `/c/` discussion form; 10 KB writeup page + all 7 comments)
- **TL;DR:** "Brute force ensembling" of 118 OOF prediction sets; Ridge assigns the weights, TabNet adds a non-linear blender.
- He calls it un-novel: gather OOFs -> ensemble -> repeat, the same pattern as his S4E9 solution.
- **Architecture:** STACK2 · stages=2 · l1=118 OOF sets (personal XGB/LGBM/CatBoost + AutoGluon/H2O/FLAML/LAMA + OpenFE-trained models
  + stacked CatBoost-OOF features) · l2=Ridge regressor on the OOF matrix (weights) + PyTorch TabNet nonlinear blender thrown back in
  as one more OOF · novel=TabNet · mod=multi-view ·
  topo=(same model set run over 3 feature views) x AutoML zoo [118 OOF cols] -> Ridge weights (+ TabNet nonlinear ensemble OOF)
  - Ridge is fitted on the OOF matrix, which makes this a stack rather than a flat average; hill-climbing reached the
    identical score with 73 models instead of 118 (so it was also tried - see Ensembling).
  - CatBoost OOF columns are also consumed as FEATURES by later models (a cascade inside level 1): the non-log CatBoost OOF
    and a multiclass-binned CatBoost OOF.
  - The TabNet blender's output is re-injected as another OOF column, adding the non-linear combination Ridge alone misses.
- **Setup:** Metric RMSLE. Public LB and CV lined up almost perfectly ("an x decrease in CV almost always resulted in around x decrease in
  public LB"), so little finesse was needed picking submissions; almost no top-10 shakeup. Worked within free Kaggle resources.
- Submission slots: not stated. Structural quirk exploited: the ORIGINAL (non-synthetic) dataset was wrongly ruled out early by the forum;
  he found it improved tuned models a lot.
- **Features:** @backpaker's contributed feature set; he ran feature importance and kept a useful subset.
- Categorisation of `healthscore` (high-cardinality) named as crucial / one of the main score improvements (credit to @swagician's breakdown).
- `autofe`/OpenFE generated a second feature set; models on OpenFE scored similar to backpacker-feature models.
- He did NOT combine the feature methods: instead the same models were trained on each different feature set to produce varied OOFs (multi-view).
- Aux stacking features: a non-log CatBoost OOF, plus a CatBoost OOF trained on a multiclass-binned target (Premium Amount 0-9 = class 0,
  10-99 = class 1, by order of the target).
- Original dataset used as extra training rows (see Gains). Exact dropped columns: not stated.
- **Models:** AutoGluon (best AutoML but overfit-prone via "stacked information leakage"; broke when a categorical version of high-card
  `healthscore` was added to the higher level models - credit @innixma / autogluon issue 2779).
- H2O and LAMA: weaker than his own models even with identical features. FLAML: surprised him positively but slow; fit once per fold (5
  concurrent notebooks) then combined predictions to stay within free resources.
- PyTorch TabNet: weak as a direct predictor but very useful as the final blender. Tensorflow DeepTables: weak here.
- Personal models: XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost behind a basic interface. Per-model hyperparameters, seeds, fold counts: not stated.
- **CV:** 5-fold (implied by the FLAML five-fold-per-notebook setup). Final CV not published; only the early original-data figure (below).
- CV-vs-LB: author states they tracked each other closely on the public LB. Author trust verdict: not stated beyond that.
- **Ensembling:** Ridge regressor as the main weight-assigner over the OOF set. Hill-climbers achieved an identical score with fewer models
  (73 vs 118). TabNet used to ensemble the predictions, then re-added as another OOF; that non-linear combination improved the overall
  ensemble. Shared-OOF usage: personal + AutoML OOFs; the public feature sets (backpacker/OpenFE) feed models, not the blender directly.
- **Post-processing:** Not stated (no threshold/clip/rank-gauss; RMSLE regression so no classification cut-off).
- **Gains (RMSLE, lower better):** (-0.004 CV) adding the original dataset: best tuned models ~1.031 -> ~1.027.
- (unquantified, "main improvement") categorising `healthscore`. (unquantified, "slight") non-log CatBoost OOF stacking feature.
  (unquantified, "small") the multiclass-binned CatBoost OOF feature.
- **Failed:** TabNet as a direct predictor ("not great"). Tensorflow DeepTables ("quite weak"). H2O and LAMA below his own models.
  AutoGluon higher-level models overfit badly once high-cardinality `healthscore` categorical was added. Early forum consensus
  (which he disproved) ruled out the original dataset.
- **Comments:** technical content is in Optimistix's (9th) reply and the author's replies:
  - Optimistix confirms he hit the same AutoGluon leaky/overfit results, notes he had been using FLAML and "sounds like it would have
    helped", and credits the author for sharing the OpenFE features that also helped his own score.
  - Author replies are congratulatory to the 4th/9th place finishers; no additional numbers.
- **Compute:** Free Kaggle resources only; FLAML parallelised across 5 fold-notebooks to fit within the free tier. Wall-clock/RAM not stated.
- **Artifacts:** (cited by the author, verbatim; not fetched)
  - https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/playground-series-s4e9/discussion/537029 (the prior approach he followed)
  - https://github.com/autogluon/autogluon/issues/2779 (stacked information leakage)
  - https://github.com/yuwei-1/Kaggle-tools/tree/main (his personal-model interface)
  - https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/playground-series-s4e12/writeups/scriptchef-rank-2-approach-brute-force-ensembling- (citation line)
  - https://www.kaggle.com/noodl35 · https://www.kaggle.com/backpaker · https://www.kaggle.com/swagician · https://www.kaggle.com/innixma
  - https://www.kaggle.com/optimistix (commenter profile link on this page)
- **Lesson:** AutoML frameworks are the cheapest way to mass-produce OOFs, but check whether the original dataset was really ruled out -
  here adding it beat a lot of stacking work.

## S4E12 — consensus recipe
- **Architecture distribution:** 1st `SINGLE` (one XGBoost, 611 engineered features) · 2nd `STACK2` (118 OOFs -> Ridge + TabNet nonlinear
  blender, CatBoost-OOF cascade features) · 9th `STACK2` (81 community OOFs -> Lasso meta-learner).
  - **winner topology: `SINGLE`** — 1st submitted one fitted model; both stacked entrants finished behind it (2nd, 9th).
- **New architectures at the board:** `TabNet` (as a level-2 nonlinear blender, not a direct predictor) in S4E12-02 is the only named
  non-stock architecture; it improved the stack but scored poorly solo. `AutoGluon` appears at both 2nd and 9th as an OOF generator /
  ensembler but never as the submitted vendor stack (so not `AUTOML`). Everything else is XGBoost/LightGBM/CatBoost + Ridge/Lasso/LogReg-
  class linear metas. No novel meta-learner won; the winner had no meta-learner at all.
- **Agreed on (3 of 3 - S4E12-01, S4E12-02, S4E12-09):** categorical encoding / feature engineering is where the score came from.
  - 1st: 7 inputs per categorical (original + 6 encodings) + 2-6 column combinations (611 features).
  - 2nd: categorising high-cardinality `healthscore` is a "main score improvement".
  - 9th: cdeotte's NAN-derived features + swagician + OpenFE ~140 features drove the member gains.
- **Agreed on (2 of 3 - S4E12-02, S4E12-09):** OpenFE/`autofe`-generated feature sets materially help.
  - 2nd trains models on the OpenFE set for diverse OOFs; 9th reports AG CV 1.045 -> 1.040 (-0.005) on noodl35's ~140 OpenFE features.
  - Not counted: 1st generated his own combination features and never uses OpenFE.
- **Agreed on (2 of 3 - S4E12-02, S4E12-09):** a fitted LINEAR meta-learner (Ridge/Lasso) as level 2; hill-climbing tried and rejected
  in favor of / matched by the linear fit.
  - 2nd: Ridge main; hill-climb identical score with 73 vs 118 models. 9th: Lasso submitted; hill-climb "several hours" beyond a few
    dozen OOFs, so dropped. Not counted: 1st uses no ensemble at all.
- **Agreed on (3 of 3 - S4E12-01, S4E12-02, S4E12-09):** GBDT families (XGBoost / LightGBM / CatBoost) are the base learners.
- **Agreed on (2 of 3 authors - S4E12-02 and the S4E12-09 author, the agreement recorded in 02's thread):** AutoGluon suffers
  stacked-information-leakage / overfitting on this data.
  - 2nd: higher-level AG models overfit once a categorical high-cardinality `healthscore` was added (autogluon issue 2779); on
    2nd's thread the 9th-place author (Optimistix) replied "I experienced the same leaky results with AG" - that agreement lives in
    S4E12-02's comment section, not in 9th's own writeup, where AG appears only as slow (~1 hour per ensembler run). Not counted: 1st never ran AutoGluon.
- **Divergences:** the winner (1st) put everything into hand-engineered features on a single XGBoost and did no ensembling, while 2nd and
  9th ran 81-118 OOF stacks with linear metas. Private-score spread is tight (9th private 1.02765; 1st and 2nd publish no private).
  - Second divergence: 2nd treated AutoML zoo as OOF factories; 9th treated the community's own notebooks as OOF factories. Same stack
    shape, different source of members.
- **Highest-leverage single trick:** using the original (non-synthetic) dataset despite early forum consensus to drop it - 2nd's tuned
  best models improved ~1.031 -> ~1.027 (-0.004 RMSLE CV). It is the biggest single gain credited to one decision; the largest raw
  quantified member-level CV delta in the set is 9th's -0.005 (OpenFE ~140 features lifting the AutoGluon member ~1.045 -> ~1.040),
  and 1st's full 611-feature build moved only -0.003 CV.
- **Nothing worked:** more blender sophistication once the OOF pool is set - hill-climbing matched Ridge (2nd) and was too slow at 81 OOFs
  (9th); the extra AG blender iteration finished too late to matter (9th).
- **Nothing worked:** TabNet as a direct predictor (2nd), Tensorflow DeepTables (2nd), H2O and LAMA below hand-built models (2nd), and
  low-CV/high-LB notebooks that 9th read as overfit and avoided trusting (he reports no explicit exclusion from the 81-OOF pool).
