- **TL;DR:** 72 OOF arrays, "a whole lotta AutoGluon", and the winning prediction is an AutoGluon stack **trained on those OOFs** — a stack of stacks, not a blender of models.
- He reached 31 submissions scoring >= 0.98512 private, the first of them two weeks before the deadline, and the top of the board was decided in the fifth decimal.
- No MCC decision threshold is reported as tuned; the only threshold-shaped move is an override rule (see Post-processing).
- **Architecture:** STACKN · stages=3 · l1=~72 OOF arrays (solo GBM/RF/XT/NN models plus AutoGluon ensemble OOFs and Hill-Climb ensemble OOFs) ·
  l2=Ridge / Hill Climbing / AutoGluon fitted on that OOF matrix · l3=AutoGluon stack trained on the level-2 outputs, then a weighted blend of two such runs (submitted) ·
  novel=none · mod=none ·
  topo=solo models → Ridge/HillClimb/AutoGluon ensembles → their OOFs re-enter the pool → AutoGluon on 72 OOFs (LB 0.98535, priv 0.98512) → weighted blend of two 0.98535 runs → 0.98537/0.98513
  - Rule 1 applied: Hill Climbing is weight selection, so HC-only runs are `FLAT`; what makes this a stack is Ridge and AutoGluon being **fitted on the OOF matrix**.
  - The stack-of-stacks evidence, in his own words: "I used Hill Climbing to decide which OOFs to use, and added the 8 which were chosen by hill climbing on top of the highest scoring AG OOF. Throwing this mix into AG" — a trained blender's output becomes an input of the next trained stage.
  - The winning artifact is therefore a vendor-managed stack sitting on top of his hand-built stack, not the biggest flat ensemble.
  - Blind blending with other people's public notebooks was a parallel, deliberate side-path used only to grab the public no. 1 spot (see Failed).
- **Setup:** ~3 million samples (his words), ~2x the variables of S4E7; 2 final slots; Kaggle 12-hour notebook limit and GPU quota as the binding constraints.
- Structural quirk exploited: the GAN injected the label noise, so a leakfree "probability of being poisonous" from the original dataset (via @siukeitin's exact solution) is a usable proxy feature.
- **Features:** deliberately thin — "I generally don't spend too much time on feature engineering, as the noisy nature of the synthetic data used in the playground series often renders it useless" (author comment).
- The one named feature: the probability of being poisonous, derived from @siukeitin's exact solution on the original dataset, "as a proxy for the signal from the original still present in the current dataset"; it boosted some models and, when it did not, "they added to the diversity of the ensemble".

### S4E8-03 · 3rd · AutoML Grandmasters · lennartpuruckerisg (Lennart Purucker, with Nick Erickson and Arjun) · LB 0.98533/not stated · CV 0.9850930506045819 (16-fold)

- **Link:** https://www.kaggle.com/c/playground-series-s4e8/discussion/523656
- **Status:** FETCHED after 2 routes: `/c/` returned a 156-byte cache shell; the same `/c/` URL with `-H "X-No-Cache: true"` returned the full post + comments (38 KB). One reply chain is collapsed on the page ("3 more replies") and the proxy cannot expand it.
- **TL;DR:** This is the AutoML Grand Prix write-up for the same month — AutoGluon fitted on a 16-fold stack over a TabRepo-derived portfolio of ~200 configs, distributed on 1000 CPUs.
- Every other score move came from patching AutoGluon's internals: 100 greedy-ensemble iterations instead of 25, and 8-decimal instead of 6-decimal tie-break rounding.
- **Architecture:** AUTOML · stages=3 (author confirms "2 levels of model and 3d level is blending") · l1=up to ~200 portfolio models per level · l2=AutoGluon stackers trained on the stack features (binary OOF = 1 column per level-1 model) · l3=greedy weighted ensemble inside AutoGluon plus their own cached-proba post-hoc ensembler ·
  novel=none · mod=none ·
  topo=noise-cleaned table → AutoGluon L1 (~200 configs) → L2 stackers → greedy weighted ensemble (100 iterations, positive weights, 8-decimal ties) → 0.98533 public
  - Rule 6 applies: the submitted stack is the vendor-managed AutoGluon one, so `AUTOML` and not `STACK2`.
  - Their post-hoc ensembler runs over cached prediction probabilities from **all** experiments, which is a second greedy selection pass on top of AutoGluon's own — the step that took them from 0.98531 to 0.98533.
  - Two AutoGluon runs feed the answer: a default one for 1 hour and the custom one for 4 hours.
- **Setup:** 24-hour Grand Prix window; ~3 million samples (per 1st's page; the team publishes no row count); 16-fold CV; the winning margin over Team AGA was "so small that Kaggle's leaderboard doesn't even visualize it".
- Structural quirk exploited: test-aware cleaning of the noisy categorical values (transductive / test-time feature engineering, as the author names it in the comments).
- **Features:** exactly one transformation, applied to a fixed `weird_columns` list of 16 categorical columns (`cap-shape`, `cap-surface`, `cap-color`, `gill-attachment`, `gill-spacing`, `gill-color`, `veil-type`, `veil-color`, `has-ring`, `ring-type`, `spore-print-color`, `habitat`, `does-bruise-or-bleed`, `stem-root`, `stem-surface`, `stem-color`): any training value not present in the test set is set to `np.nan`, and vice versa.
- "We replaced noisy observations in the data. Nothing else improved our offline cross-validation (CV) score."
- In the comments he also credits a commenter's variant: counting human-input errors per column, or flagging rows with a human-input error, restricted to actually-valid inputs.
- Encodings, GP features, dropped rows: not stated.
- **Models:** AutoGluon (installed from source, latest mainline, with distributed-computation tweaks), portfolio meta-learned from TabRepo with a **size-200** portfolio instead of the default 100; extra configurations with larger `max_bin` for LightGBM, XGBoost and CatBoost; configurations that predicted too slowly on large data removed; Linear models and KNN families added then disabled.
- Portfolio filtering to working models is published in their repo (`main.py` lines 69-84, `tabrepo_2024_custom.py`).
- Exact hyperparameters per model, seeds: not stated (delegated to the zero-shot HPO portfolio).
- **CV:** 16-fold cross-validation.
- He volunteers that the published numbers are optimistic: "these are 16-fold cross-validation scores that are slightly biased due to the weighted ensemble training on validation data and early stopping using the validation data. Also, everything but the target metric is not representative of AutoGluon's true performance."
- Validation scores for the second-to-last submission (public LB 0.98531), from his comment reply: MCC 0.9850930506045819 (target metric), ROC AUC 0.9957310456562743, Log Loss 0.07358660841538525, Balanced Accuracy 0.9926470731809423, F1 0.9932400229147786.
- CV→public offset on that artifact: 0.98509 → 0.98531 = +0.00022.
- **Ensembling:** AutoGluon's greedy weighted ensemble, monkey-patched to 100 iterations instead of the default 25 (edit `greedy_weighted_ensemble_model.py` line 22), **positive coefficients only by design** — he explains in the comments that this is to avoid overfitting, citing the greedy-ensemble-selection paper and his own work (arXiv 2307.00286).
- `EnsembleSelection.round_decimals` raised from 6 to 8 (line 112) so the tie-break could see their deltas: "to eke out an epsilon improvement".
- Separate post-hoc ensembler (`ag_post_hoc_ensembler.py`) run over cached test probas, OOF probas and predictions from every experiment, on top of a finished default AutoGluon run.
- Number of models averaged per level: "In both cases, up to a total of ~200 ... I do not have the exact number"; AutoGluon filters stacking inputs to the best N base models via `ag_args_ensemble` `max_base_models_per_type` / `max_base_models`.
- **Post-processing:** early stopping on `log_loss` while optimizing/selecting on MCC (`ag_args_fit={"stopping_metric": "log_loss"}`) — "early stopping on threshold-based metrics such as MCC can be too early"; he is explicit that he is unsure it helped, only that it improved offline CV.
- MCC decision threshold: after AutoGluon's `calibrate_decision_threshold`, "The optimal threshold after threshold calibration is `0.5`" — i.e. no shift; recorded here as post-processing per ruling 2, not a stage.
- **Gains:** (+0.00002 public, 0.98531 → 0.98533) the two Kaggle-trick patches together: 8-decimal tie-break rounding plus the custom cached-proba post-hoc ensembling — "This allowed us to just barely leapfrog Team AGA".
- (no magnitude published) the size-200 TabRepo portfolio instead of the default 100 — the page claims only "a better portfolio", with no score delta.
- (positive, magnitude not published) `log_loss` early stopping improved offline CV.
- (wall-clock, not score) dropping the slow-to-predict portfolio configs cut inference time on 3-million-row data.
- (context) 12 hours of work to beat Team AGA's early 0.98533.
- **Failed:** Linear models and KNN added to the portfolio and then disabled: "they weren't helping and, at times, took a long time to infer".
- Nothing except the noise replacement improved offline CV — their own summary of the FE search.
- Their distributed AutoGluon prototype is "mostly stable but has a few GPU-related problems".
- On published tabular architectures generally (comment): "the published results (e.g., in papers) rarely transition to actual performance in our benchmarks".
